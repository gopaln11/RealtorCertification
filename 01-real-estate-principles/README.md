# 1. Real Estate Principles

First statutory course. Do this before Finance and before Practice.

Principles is legally required for the salesperson exam. It is the vocabulary course: property, estates, title, transfer, land-use controls, and how DRE fits into the transaction. Agency, disclosures, and trust funds come back in Practice, which is the largest part of the exam, so they are filed there even though a Principles class will introduce them.

Plan on two to three weeks at about 15 hours a week. An engineering degree and an MBA do not waive this course.

This is not legal advice. The course list and the January 1, 2024 Practice rule are on the saved requirements page in the license folder: [requirements-salesperson.md](../05-license-fingerprints-broker-sponsorship/materials/requirements-salesperson.md). Source: https://www.dre.ca.gov/Examinees/RequirementsSales.html. Roadmap: [README](../README.md). Working checklist: [california-practice-path.md](../docs/california-practice-path.md).

## What still has to be purchased

The course itself. It must be 3 semester units or 4 quarter units at a regionally accredited college, or a Commissioner-approved statutory course of at least 45 hours. Confirm the sponsor on the [statutory course search](https://secure.dre.ca.gov/publicasp/cestatutory.asp) before you pay. Continuing education does not count. No Kaplan, CE Shop, PrepAgent, OnlineEd, or Allied course was downloaded.

College options DRE describes are in [education-course-availability.md](materials/education-course-availability.md). Private-school statutory courses usually do not transfer as college credit. You already have the degrees, so college credit is optional. An approved online package is the faster route if a community-college section is not actually open.
